Part 2

What gets randomized, and how much

57 item names hold ids. A single-player seed at default options places 236 of them - one per location. The split below is a real seed, not a formula.

Item groupIn a default seed ClassWhat it does
Mission unlocks18progression20 exist; the 2 starters are not in the pool
Unit unlocks21progression / useful13 of them gate something; the rest are convenience
Bonus units3usefulAirship, Bertha, Sweeper - never granted in vanilla
Progressive ERN4usefulrange 0 to 40, never required
Base generation upgrade54usefulrift lab energy per tick
Energy storage upgrade41usefulrift lab maximum store
Traps95trap6 kinds; 50 percent of leftovers by default
Build limits0not generated3 ids kept; every building starts unlimited
Total placed236

These are derived, and the seeds only check them

create_all_items is straight-line arithmetic, so the pool for any option set has a closed form - generating seeds is the double-check, not the source:

real = (20 - starters) + 21 units + 3 bonus + erns
remaining = 236 - real
traps = remaining * trap_percentage // 100
filler = remaining - traps

At defaults that is 46 real, 190 remaining, 95 traps, 95 filler. Run against all 12 configurations below and compared with what actually generated: 12 of 12 match exactly. What is NOT derivable, and so is genuinely sampled: the split of filler between the two energy upgrades and of traps between the six kinds (weighted draws), the sphere any item lands in (the output of a randomized fill), and whether fill succeeds at all. Reachability is neither - it is checked exhaustively over the graph.

The two energy upgrades are 40 percent of the pool, and both curves are wrong

95 of the 236 items are Energy Storage or Base Generation. Applying the curves the mod actually uses (EnergyRules.cs) to the counts a default seed produces, they fail in opposite directions:

itemcopiescurvewhat that delivers
Energy Storage41 +50, decaying 80% per copy 99% of its 250 ceiling reached at copy 21. Copy 30 grants 0.08, copy 41 grants 0.007 - about 20 copies are dead items
Base Generation54 +0.5/s, ramping +0.2/s per copy later copies are STRONGER. All 54 total 313 energy/sec, against a natural rate of about 3-4/s

A ramp is a reasonable curve when a player receives three of something. It is the wrong shape when they receive fifty-four: the reward for a long seed becomes an economy roughly a hundred times the intended one. The rift lab's default ceiling, for scale, is 100.

By Archipelago classification the same pool is 31 progression, 110 useful, 95 trap, 0 filler. The 31 is exactly 18 mission unlocks plus the 13 items that appear in an access rule - nothing is marked progression that gates nothing, which would distort the sphere structure.

One row of that table used to be luck. It is now fixed.

starter_missions: 1 passed here, but re-rolling it 300 times showed it failing to generate about 12 percent of the time - a FillError, on seeds that were all provably winnable. Found because it turned a new test into an intermittent CI failure. Two causes, both now closed:

causeratefix
Two early items, one slot 12% to 1.3% every eligible starter has exactly ONE cache reachable with nothing; the world asked for both a mission unlock and the early weapon
A scarce slot spent on an item that opens nothing 1.3% to 0% a lone Factory is half of the Greenar pair; the fill does not look ahead

The fix is bootstrap_opening, in pre_fill: while the opening is too narrow to survive a wasted placement, the world places items itself, drawn at random from those that actually open something. It is not a script - 1 to 6 placements across 40 seeds, eight different items seen first - and it stops as soon as there is slack.

It runs only where it is needed. With another game in the multiworld the funnel stops being one: the fill parks Creeper World 4 unlocks in that world and fills its opening with that game's items. Running the bootstrap there is not free either - it drove foreign items in the opening from 7 to 0 - so it stands down unless every player is playing this game.

The two "degrades" rows are the interesting ones

Both are unfillable preferences, and neither is allowed to fail generation. Zeroing every trap weight turns those slots into useful items. Zeroing every filler weight falls back to a single kind - and that seed came out 95 energy storage, 0 base generation, which is the new fallback working. It used to fall back to build limits, so removing those items would have quietly turned the safety net into 95 items that do nothing.

Part 4

Reachability and cross-language agreement

Logic

  • PASS21 regions, 256 locations (236 checks + 20 events)
  • PASSevery location reachable holding all items
  • PASSgoal satisfiable
  • PASSevery seed produced a playthrough, so every seed is beatable in order

Apworld vs. the game mod

  • PASSall 20 mission titles identical
  • PASSfinal mission agrees (Founders, not Ever After)
  • PASSper-mission objective slots match the location set
  • PASSall 7 trap names exist in TrapRules

The third of those is worth its own sentence: the mod's objective table was previously pinned against the live game's own map icons, and the apworld's location counts against the campaign survey - but nothing checked the two against each other. A disagreement there would show as a mission map drawing an icon for a category with no checks, or a check with no icon. They agree on all 20.

Missile Launcher is the latest-arriving item in the game

Median sphere 9 of 13.6, three quarters of the way in, in the last 40 percent of 14 seeds out of 20 and dead last in 3 - very slightly worse than Sprayer, which was previously the record holder.

That falls out of the logic rather than the fill. Missile Launcher appears in exactly one rule, DEFENSIVE = ["Sniper", "Missile Launcher"], which applies only under logic_difficulty: casual from mission 6 onward - and even there it is an OR with Sniper, so holding a Sniper makes it redundant. Under default logic it gates nothing at all, so nothing ever pulls it forward.

Sniper is the useful contrast: it looks like the same kind of item but sits at median 58 percent, because The Compound genuinely requires one (MISSION_EXTRA[16]) and that single rule is enough to give it a place in the sphere structure.

The opener is a clean coin flip: Cannon 10, Mortar 10

Cannon and Mortar are one OR group (OFFENSE), so logic needs either and never both. Whichever lands early is the opening weapon, and the other is logically redundant for the rest of that seed.

An earlier pass over these same seeds reported 13-7 and it was an artefact of the method: it read the spoiler's playthrough, which lists only the items needed to win, so it cannot see the redundant half of an OR pair at all. Measuring real placement depth gives 10-10. Reversing the pair to ["Mortar", "Cannon"] and regenerating produced identical seeds either way, so list order does not choose the winner.

Choosing the opener costs you the other weapon

early_weapon now makes the coin flip a choice. It is placement, not logic - a rule saying a mission needs a mortar specifically would be false wherever a cannon also works - so it uses Archipelago's early_items, the same mechanism that already forces a second mission unlock early.

It costs less than a first look suggests, and getting that wrong is instructive. Comparing Cannon-under-unforced against Cannon-under-mortar shows a big regression - but under unforced Cannon opens half the seeds, and under mortar it never does, so that compares two different jobs. Comparing BY ROLE, 20 seeds each:

settingopening weaponsecond weaponsecond in final sphere

mortarmedian 1 (always)median 8, 67% in2 / 20

The second weapon lands about two thirds of the way in whatever you choose. That is a property of an OR pair - the loser is redundant for the whole seed and nothing pulls a redundant item forward - and it was already true before the option existed. What forcing buys is a weapon in the very first sphere instead of somewhere in the first four; the only real cost is the extreme tail, 0 seeds in 20 becoming 2 or 3.

The option is mortar, cannon or random, defaulting to random. random is not defined by the world: Archipelago accepts it for any choice and resolves it per seed, and defining it would raise - Options.py asserts that "Choice option 'random' cannot be manually assigned". Over 100 default seeds the split came out 51 Mortar to 49 Cannon.

A fourth value that reproduced the first row exactly was built and then removed. It worked, but once the by-role numbers showed the whole difference was a weapon in sphere 1 versus sphere 1 to 4, it could not justify the space it took up in every player's yaml.

"Mortar, then Sprayer, then Cannon, all by mid" is not available

Archipelago gives a world two placement controls: force an item into sphere 1 (early_items), or pin it to a chosen location (place_locked_item). There is nothing for "somewhere in the middle". Bounding the second weapon to mid-game would mean pinning it, which takes it out of the shuffle and stops it ever travelling to another player in a multiworld. That trade has not been made.

Sprayer is the latest-arriving of the three and always has been - median 75 percent of the way in, in the last 40 percent of 14 seeds out of 20. It is in no rule deliberately (sprayers burn bluite, and every bluite map was judged short of the resource for a sprayer-only run), so it is never progression and never pulled forward. It could be nudged early as a non-logic item without asserting anything false - that lever exists and is unused.

Part 6

What this audit does not cover

Still the big one: no full playthrough

Everything above is structural. It proves seeds generate, fill, and are beatable in principle, and that the mod and the apworld agree on names. It says nothing about whether the result is fun: trap frequency at 95 items a seed, energy-item pacing at 95 upgrades, and how early the casual tier lands are all unmeasured in play.

Economy is outside logic on purpose

Miner gates nothing. Tower of Darkness was the suspected exception and was played without one to check: "very doable with no miners". A test pins it.

Two items exist but are not generated

Emitter Overdrive (dead on a third of the campaign) and the three build-limit items (dead on all of it). Ids retained so seeds and clients stay compatible.
